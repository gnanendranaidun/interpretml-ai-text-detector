# Additional papers for the InterpretML presentation

26. **Krishna, S. et al. (2024). “The Disagreement Problem in Explainable Machine Learning: A Practitioner's Perspective.” Transactions on Machine Learning Research.** [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=jESY2WTZCe). Motivates reporting agreement between LIME and Kernel SHAP instead of assuming a single ranking is definitive.
27. **Laberge, G., Pequignot, Y. B., Marchand, M., & Khomh, F. (2024). “Tackling the XAI Disagreement Problem with Regional Explanations.” AISTATS.** [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/laberge24a.html). Shows why aggregate comparisons across groups can complement individual explanations.
28. **Hill, D., Masoomi, A., Torop, M., Ghimire, S., & Dy, J. (2024). “Boundary-Aware Uncertainty for Feature Attribution Explainers.” AISTATS.** [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/hill24a.html). Distinguishes uncertainty in explanations from uncertainty in model outputs.
29. **Covert, I., & Lee, S.-I. (2021). “Improving KernelSHAP: Practical Shapley Value Estimation Using Linear Regression.” AISTATS.** [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/covert21a.html). Explains why coalition sampling and estimation variance matter when reading Kernel SHAP scores.
30. **Gupta, C., & Ramdas, A. (2021). “Distribution-Free Calibration Guarantees for Histogram Binning without Sample Splitting.” ICML.** [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/gupta21b.html). Places the calibration slide's bin-level reliability summary in context.
31. **Shan, Z., Lee, Y., & Hao, S. (2026). “AI Writers Have a Consistent Stylometric Footprint, but AI Editors Do Not.” EMNLP.** [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1725/). Motivates separating the study's generation labels from AI-assisted editing when interpreting stylometry.
32. **Chouraqui, G., Cohen, L., Einziger, G., & Leman, L. (2022). “A geometric method for improved uncertainty estimation in real-time.” UAI.** [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/chouraqui22a.html). Motivates auditing model scores as empirical probabilities rather than reading the largest score as calibrated confidence.
